# Is this really Dupaco? How to tell if a call, text or email is legit

If you've asked yourself any of these questions, you're not alone.

Scammers have become increasingly skilled at making emails, texts, phone calls and even mailings look real. They often use real company names, familiar logos and convincing stories to create a sense of urgency.

The good news? You don't need to be a fraud expert to spot the warning signs. Here's what to know when someone claims to be Dupaco—and what to do before you respond.

### Is it really Dupaco?

If you're unsure whether a call, text, email or mailing is really from Dupaco:

- Don't click links or respond right away.

- Don't call a phone number included in the message.

- Never share your password, PIN or verification code.

- Contact Dupaco directly at 800-373-7600 to verify the communication.

When in doubt, verify first.

## First things first: Don't panic

Scammers want you to feel one thing above all else: Urgency.

They might claim:

- Your account has been compromised.

- Your debit or credit card has suspicious activity.

- Your online banking has been locked.

- Your mortgage requires immediate action.

- A payment is overdue.

- You need to verify personal information right away.

The message may arrive by:

- Phone call

- Text message

- Email

- Letter or postcard

- Social media message

- Website pop-up

While the details change, the goal is usually the same: Get you to act before you have time to think.

Scammers often rely on what's known as [amygdala hijack](https://www.dupaco.com/learn/blog/what-amygdala-hijack-is-and-what-it-has-to-do-with-fraud), triggering fear, urgency or panic so you react emotionally instead of slowing down to verify the situation.

> Take a breath. Slow down. Verify first.

## Ask yourself these questions first

Whether you're looking at an email, answering a phone call or reading a letter, these questions can help you quickly spot a potential scam.

#### \|1\| Are they creating urgency?

Scammers often pressure people to act immediately.

Common examples include:

- "Act now."

- "Response needed."

- "Your account will be closed."

- "Your access has been suspended."

- "Time-sensitive notice."

Legitimate organizations may communicate deadlines. Scammers use pressure to keep you from stopping to think.

#### \|2\| Are they asking you to take immediate action?

Be cautious if you're being directed to:

- Click a link

- Call an unfamiliar phone number

- Download a file

- Share personal information

Always navigate directly to [Dupaco.com](https://www.dupaco.com/) or call a trusted phone number yourself.

![Infographic with an hourglass and text that reads: 'Buy some time. In stressful situations, practice creating time and space to think. Pausing, even if it's just for a minute, will allow your brain to better process the situation.'](https://www.dupaco.com/hs-fs/hubfs/Graphics/Infographics/Fraud%20and%20security/How-to-Spot-Scams-Tip-Buy-Time-Graphic.jpg?width=676&height=338&name=How-to-Spot-Scams-Tip-Buy-Time-Graphic.jpg)

#### \|3\| Are they asking for information Dupaco shouldn't need?

This is one of the biggest red flags.

Dupaco will never ask for:

- ❌ Your Shine password

- ❌ Your debit card PIN

- ❌ Your full debit or credit card number
- ❌ Your two-step verification code

- ❌ Password reset codes sent to your phone or email

- ❌ Sensitive information we already have

As Jim Bemboom, assistant vice president, card services at Dupaco, puts it:

> "Giving out your PIN is like giving someone cash. We will never ask for that."

If anyone claiming to be Dupaco asks for this information, stop the conversation and contact us directly.

You’re the only one who can change your PIN. If you’ve shared your number, you should immediately change it. Call Dupaco at 800-373-7600 for steps on how to do so.

## Did Dupaco call me about fraud?

This is a common question we hear.

Unfortunately, scammers sometimes pretend they're calling to help you protect you from fraud.

Through a tactic called caller ID spoofing, fraudsters can make it appear as though they're calling from a legitimate phone number—even a Dupaco number.

They may claim:

- Your card has suspicious transactions.

- Your online banking has been compromised.

- They need to verify activity on your account.

Then they begin asking questions.

> “They make it look like they’re verifying your recent card transactions because they logged into your account with your password," Bemboom said.

Remember: Dupaco will not ask for your PIN, online banking password or two-step verification code.

“Fraudsters will do their best to lull you into a sense of security to get you to let your guard down,” Bemboom said.

If you're unsure whether a fraud call is legitimate, hang up and call Dupaco directly at 800-373-7600.

## Is this email or text legitimate?

Fraudulent emails and texts are often designed to look nearly identical to legitimate messages.

Before clicking any link:

- Check the sender's email address or phone number carefully.

- Hover over links (without clicking) to see where they lead.

- Look for unusual wording, poor grammar or generic greetings like "Dear Client" or "Dear Valued Customer."

- Go directly to [Dupaco.com](https://www.dupaco.com/) instead of using links in the message.

### Dupaco tip

**Remember:** A professional-looking logo doesn't prove a message is legitimate. Scammers can copy logos, colors and branding.

## What about letters or postcards?

Some mailings can appear surprisingly convincing because they reference information related to your mortgage, property or other financial accounts.

In some cases, companies use publicly available information to send mass mailings. In others, scammers may try to create a sense of urgency to get you to respond.

Be cautious if a mailing:

- Uses urgent language

- Directs you to call an unfamiliar number

- Requests personal information

- Includes private information on a postcard
- Includes suspicious websites

If you're unsure whether a mailing is connected to your relationship with Dupaco, contact us directly before responding.

## What if I clicked or shared information?

First: You're not the first person this has happened to, and you won't be the last.

Scammers are good at what they do. But acting quickly may help limit the damage.

#### Take these steps immediately:

- Contact Dupaco at 800-373-7600.

- Change affected passwords.

- Review recent account activity.

- Enable or verify two-factor authentication.

- Monitor your credit in [Shine](https://www.dupaco.com/shine).

- Report unauthorized transactions.

- Run a malware or antivirus scan if you clicked suspicious links or downloaded attachments.

The sooner you act, the more options you may have to help protect your accounts.

### Fraud spotlight: Account takeover

One of the biggest risks after responding to a phishing email, scam text or fraudulent phone call is something called **account takeover**. This happens when a criminal gains access to one of your online accounts, changes your login credentials and locks you out.

Warning signs include:

- Password reset emails you didn't request

- Login alerts from unfamiliar devices

- Being locked out of your account

- Transactions you don't recognize

- Friends or family receiving strange messages from you

**If you notice any of these signs, contact Dupaco immediately and update your passwords.**

## 5 habits to protect yourself year-round

Fraud trends change constantly, but these habits are always in style.

#### \|1\| Use strong, unique passwords

Avoid reusing passwords across multiple accounts. Learn how to [create stronger passwords](https://www.dupaco.com/learn/blog/how-to-spring-clean-your-passwords).

#### \|2\| Turn on multifactor authentication

This adds an extra layer of protection if a password is compromised.

#### \|3\| Set up account alerts

Transaction and login alerts can help you spot fraud quickly.

Check your eNotifier settings within [Shine](https://www.dupaco.com/shine) to make sure you're getting the alerts you want.

#### \|4\| Monitor your credit

Regularly reviewing your credit can help you identify potential identity theft sooner.

Dupaco members can access credit monitoring tools and alerts right within [Shine](https://www.dupaco.com/shine).

#### \|5\| Pause before you act

A few seconds of verification can prevent a major headache later.

When something feels urgent, that's often the best time to slow down.
