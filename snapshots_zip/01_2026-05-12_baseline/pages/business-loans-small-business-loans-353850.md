- The result assumes the interest rate stays the same and repayments are made on time every month.
- Fees, charges and changes in the interest rate are not included.
- The estimate is a guide only and does not replace personal financial advice.

By understanding your inputs and experimenting with scenarios, you can shape your loan strategy to lower costs, better match your income cycles, and build confidence before signing an agreement.

There are three core levers you can adjust to improve your loan outcome:

1. Loan term (length)
2. Interest rate
3. Repayment behavior (frequency and extra payments)

### Match the term to your cash flow

The loan term (how many years you take to pay back) is the biggest driver of payment size and total interest.

- Longer term with smaller monthly payments=more total interest
- Shorter term with larger payments=less total interest

### Practical tips

- If you have steady cash flow, choose a shorter term to shave thousands of dollars off total interest.
- Example: A $150,000 loan at 7% over 10 years will incur less interest over 7 years. If your revenue is reliable, the calculator will show you how much interest you save by tightening the term.
- Compare scenarios side-by-side

This clarity helps you choose a term that doesn’t squeeze your operational cash flow.

### Lower your interest rate

The interest rate has a massive impact on the total cost of the loan.

Actions that can lower your rate:

#### Improve your credit profile

- Clear outstanding debts
- Ensure financial statements are accurate and clean
- Get personal or business credit reports and correct errors

#### Strengthen your application

- Provide a robust business plan and cash-flow forecast
- Show consistent revenue history
- Use collateral if appropriate

### Final Thought

The key to improving your loan outcomes isn’t just filling out a calculator once, it’s using the calculator as a planning tool. By systematically exploring terms, rates, and repayment behaviors, you transform an abstract number into a financial strategy that fits your cash flow goals and preserves business liquidity.
