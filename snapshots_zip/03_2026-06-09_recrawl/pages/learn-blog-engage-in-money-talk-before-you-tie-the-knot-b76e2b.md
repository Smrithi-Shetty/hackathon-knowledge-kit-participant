# Engage in money talk before you tie the knot

[Family & Relationships](https://www.dupaco.com/learn/blog/tag/family-relationships)

![Engage in money talk before you tie the knot](https://www.dupaco.com/hubfs/wordpress-migration/websize-Copy-3.jpg)

Getting ready to tie the knot?

The more you and your partner can talk openly about your finances before the big day, the easier it will be to merge your lives and money.

Here are six financial steps to take before you say, “I do”:

## **\|1\| Talk about your assets and debts**

Be honest with each other about the debts you carry, including student loans, credit cards and other loan payments.

These discussions can be tricky to navigate, so it can be helpful to involve a financial expert. A free Dupaco Credit History Lesson can help you review your debts, create a payoff plan that works for you both and even teach you how to drive up your credit scores.

[Request your free Credit History Lesson >](https://www.dupaco.com/about/member-services)

## **\|2\| Identify your shared financial goals**

What are your hopes and dreams together?

Do you want to [buy a house](https://www.dupaco.com/personal/loans/home-loans)? Do you dream of traveling? When do you want to retire?

Talk openly about your shared goals, so you know what you’re [saving](https://www.dupaco.com/personal/savings-account) for and working toward together.

## **\|3\| Know each other’s money mindsets**

When you know your money personalities, it can help you have a better relationship with your money and each other. Talk about your money habits.

What will each of your financial roles in your relationship be? Having honest conversations about how you spend your money could help you work together as a couple.

## **\|4\| Decide how to set up your accounts**

Be open about your expectations when it comes to joint or separate financial accounts.

Some couples merge everything, while others keep their finances mostly separate.

It comes down to what works best for each couple.

Just because you’re getting married doesn’t mean you need to start sharing a checking account. If you have a budget that works for you, who’s to say that has to change when you get married?

But life happens, and it’s important to re-evaluate how your finances are allocated whenever you experience a life-changing event.

## **\|5\| Create a budget**

Create a [budget](https://www.dupaco.com/learn/master-your-money/budgeting-saving/) that works for your finances.

A free Dupaco Money Makeover could help you navigate this process—and even help you weigh the pros and cons of how to best merge your money.

During this process, you can even set up separate [You-Name-It Savings accounts](https://www.dupaco.com/personal/savings-account) for all your budget items, from [insurance](https://www.dupaco.com/personal/insurance) to cell phone payments.

A Money Makeover could be a great thing to do together as a couple to help you decide who’s going to be on what accounts.
